1Functions and transformations · spec MM8.2 · Paper 1 style · ★☆☆

The graph of \(y = f(x)\) is transformed by the sequence: reflect in the \(x\)-axis, then translate \(2\) units in the positive \(y\)-direction, then reflect in the \(y\)-axis. The resulting graph has equation

  1. A\(y = f(-x) - 2\)
  2. B\(y = 2 - f(x)\)
  3. C\(y = -f(-x + 2)\)
  4. D\(y = 2 - f(-x)\)
  5. E\(y = -f(x - 2)\)
  6. F\(y = -f(-x) - 2\)
Worked solution

Reflect in \(x\)-axis: \(y = -f(x)\). Translate up \(2\): \(y = -f(x) + 2\). Reflect in \(y\)-axis (replace \(x\) by \(-x\)): \(y = 2 - f(-x)\).

How many times does the graph of \(y = x^3 - x^2 - 6x\) cross or touch the \(x\)-axis?

  1. A\(3\)
  2. B\(1\)
  3. C\(0\)
  4. D\(2\)

4Identifying errors in proofs · spec Err2 · Paper 1 style · ★☆☆

A student solves \(x^2 = 4x\).

  1. IDivide both sides by \(x\): \(x = 4\).
  2. IISo the only solution is \(x = 4\).

Which of the following is correct?

  1. AThe first error is on line I: dividing by \(x\) discards the solution \(x = 0\).
  2. BThe solution is completely correct.
  3. CThe first error is on line II.
  4. DThe first error is on line I: \(x^2 \div x = 2x\).
  5. EThe first error is on line I: it should give \(x = 2\).
Worked solution

Factorise instead: \(x(x - 4) = 0\) gives \(x = 0\) or \(x = 4\).

5Polynomials, factor and remainder theorems · spec MM1.6 · Paper 2 style · ★☆☆

\(p(x)\) is a polynomial. \(P\): '\(p(2) = 0\)'. \(Q\): '\((x - 2)^2\) is a factor of \(p(x)\)'. Which one of the following is true?

  1. AP is necessary and sufficient for Q.
  2. BP is necessary but not sufficient for Q.
  3. CP is sufficient but not necessary for Q.
  4. DP is neither necessary nor sufficient for Q.
Worked solution

If \((x-2)^2\) divides \(p\) then certainly \(p(2) = 0\), so \(P\) is necessary for \(Q\). But \(p(x) = x - 2\) has \(p(2) = 0\) without a repeated factor, so \(P\) is not sufficient.

6Chains of reasoning · spec Prf5 · Paper 1 style · ★☆☆

In a class every student studies at least one of Maths and Physics. \(70\%\) study Maths and \(60\%\) study Physics. A student is picked at random. What is the probability they study both?

  1. A\(0.42\)
  2. B\(0.2\)
  3. C\(0.1\)
  4. D\(0.3\)
  5. E\(0.5\)
  6. Fit cannot be determined
Worked solution

\(P(M \text{ or } P) = 1 = 0.7 + 0.6 - P(\text{both})\), so \(P(\text{both}) = 0.3\).

7The logic of arguments · spec Arg1 · Paper 1 style · ★☆☆

Consider the statement: **if \(n\) is a multiple of \(6\), then \(n\) is a multiple of \(3\).** Which of the following is its contrapositive?

  1. AIf \(n\) is not a multiple of \(3\), then \(n\) is not a multiple of \(6\).
  2. BIf \(n\) is a multiple of \(3\), then \(n\) is a multiple of \(6\).
  3. CIf \(n\) is not a multiple of \(6\), then \(n\) is not a multiple of \(3\).
  4. D\(n\) is a multiple of \(6\) if and only if \(n\) is a multiple of \(3\).
  5. EIf \(n\) is a multiple of \(3\), then \(n\) is not a multiple of \(6\).
Worked solution

The contrapositive of 'if \(A\) then \(B\)' is 'if not \(B\) then not \(A\)'. It is logically equivalent to the original; B is the converse and C is the inverse.

8Mathematical proof · spec Prf1 · Paper 1 style · ★☆☆

To prove **'if \(n^2\) is even then \(n\) is even'** by contradiction, which assumption should be made?

  1. A\(n\) is odd.
  2. B\(n^2\) is even and \(n\) is odd.
  3. C\(n^2\) is even.
  4. D\(n^2\) is odd.
  5. E\(n\) is even and \(n^2\) is odd.
Worked solution

Assume the hypothesis holds and the conclusion fails, then derive a contradiction: \(n\) odd gives \(n^2\) odd, contradicting \(n^2\) even.

9The logic of arguments · spec Arg4 · Paper 1 style · ★★☆

A statement reads: **for every positive integer \(n\), there is a prime \(p\) with \(n < p < 2n\).** Which of the following is its negation?

  1. AFor every positive integer \(n\), there is a prime \(p\) with \(p \leq n\) or \(p \geq 2n\).
  2. BThere is a positive integer \(n\) such that no prime \(p\) satisfies \(n < p < 2n\).
  3. CThere is a positive integer \(n\) and a prime \(p\) with \(p \leq n\) or \(p \geq 2n\).
  4. DThere is a prime \(p\) such that for every positive integer \(n\), \(n < p < 2n\) fails.
  5. EFor every positive integer \(n\), no prime \(p\) satisfies \(n < p < 2n\).
Worked solution

Negate the quantifiers in turn: 'for every \(n\), there exists \(p\) with ...' becomes 'there exists \(n\) such that for every prime \(p\), not ...', i.e. no prime lies strictly between \(n\) and \(2n\).

Answer B · The logic of arguments (Arg4) · question LGC-18
10Chains of reasoning · spec Prf5 · Paper 1 style · ★★☆

A rectangle has integer side lengths and its area (in cm\(^2\)) is numerically equal to its perimeter (in cm). How many such rectangles are there (counting \(a \times b\) and \(b \times a\) once)?

  1. A\(4\)
  2. B\(3\)
  3. C\(0\)
  4. D\(1\)
  5. E\(2\)
  6. Finfinitely many
Worked solution

\(ab = 2a + 2b \Rightarrow (a - 2)(b - 2) = 4\): \((a - 2, b - 2) = (1, 4)\) or \((2, 2)\), giving \(3 \times 6\) and \(4 \times 4\).

11Binomial expansion · spec MM2.4 · Paper 2 style · ★★☆

The expansion of \(\left(2x - \dfrac{1}{x^2}\right)^{9}\) contains a term in \(x^{k}\) for each of which of the following values of \(k\)?

  1. I\(k = 0\)
  2. II\(k = 3\)
  3. III\(k = -6\)
  1. Anone of them
  2. BI only
  3. CII only
  4. DIII only
  5. EI and II only
  6. FI and III only
  7. GII and III only
  8. HI, II and III
Worked solution

General term: \(\binom9r (2x)^{9-r}(-x^{-2})^r\) has power \(9 - r - 2r = 9 - 3r\), which takes values \(9, 6, 3, 0, -3, \ldots, -18\). \(k = 0\) needs \(r = 3\): yes. \(k = 3\) needs \(r = 2\): yes. \(k = -6\) needs \(r = 5\): yes. All three.

12Chains of reasoning · spec Prf5 · Paper 1 style · ★★☆

Five friends each shake hands with some of the others (each pair shakes hands at most once). Which of the following is impossible?

  1. AEvery friend shakes hands with exactly two others.
  2. BEvery friend shakes hands with exactly four others.
  3. CThe numbers of handshakes made are \(1, 1, 2, 2, 2\).
  4. DThe numbers of handshakes made by the five friends are \(4, 4, 4, 4, 1\).
  5. EExactly one friend shakes no hands at all.
Worked solution

If four people each shook hands with all four others, everyone shook hands with everyone, so the fifth person also made \(4\) handshakes, not \(1\). The others can happen: B is a pentagon of handshakes, C is everyone with everyone, D is a chain, E leaves one person out.

Answer D · Chains of reasoning (Prf5) · question RSN-03
13Statistics · spec M6.3 · Paper 2 style · ★★☆

A set of five distinct positive integers has median \(10\) and mean \(10\). Which of the following statements must be true?

  1. IThe largest value is at least \(12\).
  2. IIThe smallest value is at most \(8\).
  3. IIIThe range is at least \(4\).
  1. Anone of them
  2. BI only
  3. CII only
  4. DIII only
  5. EI and II only
  6. FI and III only
  7. GII and III only
  8. HI, II and III
Worked solution

The two values above the median are distinct integers greater than \(10\), so at least \(11\) and \(12\) (I); similarly the two below are at most \(9\) and \(8\) (II); hence the range is at least \(12 - 8 = 4\) (III).

14Simultaneous equations and inequalities · spec MM1.5 · Paper 2 style · ★★☆

Given that \(|2x - 3| < 5\), which of the following statements is/are true?

  1. I\(-1 < x < 4\)
  2. II\(x^2 < 16\)
  3. III\(x > -1\)
  1. Anone of them
  2. BI only
  3. CII only
  4. DIII only
  5. EI and II only
  6. FI and III only
  7. GII and III only
  8. HI, II and III
Worked solution

\(-5 < 2x - 3 < 5 \Rightarrow -1 < x < 4\), so I is true and III follows from I. For II: every \(x\) in \((-1, 4)\) has \(x^2 < 16\) (the largest possible \(|x|\) is just under \(4\)), so II is also true.

  2. BIf you revise, then you will pass.
  3. CYou will pass if you revise.

16Identifying errors in proofs · spec Err1 · Paper 1 style · ★★☆

Claim: for all positive real \(x\), \(x + \dfrac{1}{x} \geq 2\).

  1. ISuppose \(x + \dfrac1x \geq 2\).
  2. IIMultiply by \(x > 0\): \(x^2 + 1 \geq 2x\).
  3. IIISo \(x^2 - 2x + 1 \geq 0\), i.e. \((x - 1)^2 \geq 0\), which is true.
  4. IVHence the claim is proved.

Which of the following best describes the argument?

  1. AThe argument assumes what it is trying to prove (line I); it would be valid if the steps were reversed, starting from \((x - 1)^2 \geq 0\).
  2. BThe argument is completely correct as written.
  3. CThe first error is on line II: multiplying by \(x\) reverses the inequality.
  4. DThe first error is on line III.
  5. EThe claim is false.
Worked solution

Starting from the conclusion and reaching a truth does not prove the conclusion unless every step is reversible and the argument is presented in the forward direction. Here the steps are reversible, so the repair is to write it backwards.

17Mathematical proof · spec Prf1 · Paper 1 style · ★★☆

Here is a proof that there are infinitely many primes, with the steps out of order.

  1. IThen \(N\) leaves remainder \(1\) on division by each of \(p_1, \ldots, p_n\), so no \(p_i\) divides \(N\).
  2. IISuppose there are only finitely many primes \(p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n\), and let \(N = p_1 p_2 \cdots p_n + 1\).
  3. IIISo \(N\) has a prime factor that is not in the list, which is a contradiction.
  4. IVBut \(N > 1\), so \(N\) has at least one prime factor.

Which is the correct order?

  1. AII, I, IV, III
  2. BII, III, I, IV
  3. CI, II, IV, III
  4. DII, I, III, IV
  5. EIV, II, I, III
Worked solution

Define \(N\) (II), show no listed prime divides it (I), note that \(N\) nevertheless has some prime factor (IV), and conclude that this factor is a new prime (III). The conclusion III cannot come before both I and IV have been established.

18Graphs of functions · spec MM8.6 · Paper 2 style · ★★☆

\(p(x)\) is a polynomial of degree \(4\) with real coefficients. Which of the following statements must be true?

  1. I\(p(x) = 0\) has at least one real root.
  2. II\(p(x) = 0\) has at most four real roots.
  3. IIIThe graph of \(y = p(x)\) has at least one turning point.
  1. Anone of them
  2. BI only
  3. CII only
  4. DIII only
  5. EI and II only
  6. FI and III only
  7. GII and III only
  8. HI, II and III
Worked solution

I fails: \(x^4 + 1\) has no real roots. II: a degree-4 polynomial has at most 4 roots. III: an even-degree polynomial tends to the same infinity at both ends, so it must turn at least once.

20Straight lines · spec MM3.1 · Paper 2 style · ★★★

The line through \((a, 0)\) and \((0, b)\), with \(a, b > 0\), passes through \((2, 3)\). Which of the following statements is/are true?

  1. I\(\dfrac{2}{a} + \dfrac{3}{b} = 1\)
  2. II\(a > 2\) and \(b > 3\)
  3. IIIThe smallest possible value of \(ab\) is \(24\)
  1. Anone of them
  2. BI only
  3. CII only
  4. DIII only
  5. EI and II only
  6. FI and III only
  7. GII and III only
  8. HI, II and III
Worked solution

The line is \(\frac xa + \frac yb = 1\), so I holds. Since \(\frac2a < 1\) and \(\frac3b < 1\), II holds. \(b = \frac{3a}{a-2}\), so \(ab = \frac{3a^2}{a-2}\); setting \(a = 2 + t\) gives \(\frac{3(2+t)^2}{t} = 3\left(\frac4t + 4 + t\right) \geq 3(4 + 4) = 24\) by AM-GM (equality at \(t = 2\), \(a = 4\), \(b = 6\)). III holds.
